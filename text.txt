The Fox and the River
A small red fox lived by a wide river. Every morning the fox went down to the river to drink, and every morning the river said the same thing: "Slow down, little fox. The water will be here tomorrow."
The fox did not slow down. The fox ran to the river, and the fox ran back to the wood, and the fox ran all day long. "I have no time," said the fox. "The day is short and the wood is big."
One day a gray bird sat on a stone in the river. The bird sang a song, and the song was long and sweet. The fox sat down on the bank to drink, and the fox heard the song. The fox did not run. The fox sat on the bank and listened, and the sun went over the wood, and the river ran on and on.
"Sing it again," said the fox. The bird sang it again. "Sing it again," said the fox, and the bird sang it again, and again, and again, until the sun went down behind the hill.
"You sat all day," said the river. "Was the day short?" "The day was long," said the fox, "and it was a good day."
The next morning the fox went down to the river to drink. The bird was on the stone, and the bird sang the song. The fox sat on the bank and listened. The river ran on and on, and the wood was big, and the day was long.
A small brown hare ran by. "Run, fox!" said the hare. "The day is short and the wood is big!" "Sit down, little hare," said the fox. "The water will be here tomorrow."
The hare sat down on the bank by the fox. The bird sang the song, and the river ran on, and the sun went over the wood. The fox and the hare sat all day, and it was a good day.
Now every morning the fox and the hare go down to the river to drink, and the bird sings, and the river says the same thing it said before: "Slow down. The water will be here tomorrow."
